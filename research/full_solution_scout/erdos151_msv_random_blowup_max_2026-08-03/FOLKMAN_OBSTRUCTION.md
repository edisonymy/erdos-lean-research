# Universal Folkman obstruction to the finite `T_3` MSV lane

**Status (3 August 2026).** Proved below and independently reconstructed
against the audited Folkman reduction in
[`../../erdos151/general/GENERAL.md`](../../erdos151/general/GENERAL.md) and
[`../../erdos151/general/AUDIT.md`](../../erdos151/general/AUDIT.md). The
explicit colouring is also generated by `msv_s3_probe.py` and checked from an
exported edge list by `independent_verify.py`. This is a campaign route-closure
result, not a solution of Erdős #151 and not a novelty claim.

The finite probe implements Definition 2.1 of Morris--Sahasrabudhe--Verstraëte,
[*On the Erdős--Rogers function*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16118), for
`s=3`: its indexed owner graphs are blow-ups of balanced complete tripartite
graphs. The following observation does not depend on any asymptotic parameter,
probability estimate, random seed, or deletion optimizer.

## Theorem

Let `J_1,...,J_t` be indexed complete tripartite graphs on subsets of a common
vertex set `V`. Form their union and perform the two MSV deletions:

1. delete every generated edge `xy` for which `x,y` lie together in the
   vertex sets of at least two distinct indexed owners;
2. from every remaining triangle not jointly contained in one owner, delete
   at least one of its edges.

Call the resulting graph `G`. Then `G` has a red--blue edge-colouring with no
monochromatic triangle. Consequently `G` does not edge-arrow `(3,3)`. By the
audited Folkman reduction,

```text
beta(G) >= H(|V|).
```

In particular, **no output of the theorem-faithful `s=3`, `J=T_3(q)` MSV
construction can be a counterexample to Erdős #151**, even if the indexed
copies, partitions, and second-deletion choices are optimized adversarially.

## Proof

After the first deletion, each surviving edge has a unique indexed owner.
Indeed, it was generated by at least one owner, while the literal first
deletion removes it if its endpoints co-belong to two owner vertex sets.

Every triangle surviving the second deletion lies jointly in an owner. Pick
one of its edges. The edge's unique owner must be that triangle's owner, and
all three triangle vertices therefore lie in this same complete tripartite
graph. The triangle uses one vertex from each of its three parts.

For each indexed owner separately, label its parts `0,1,2`. Colour its
surviving edges between parts `0--1` and `1--2` red, and those between parts
`0--2` blue. Since every surviving edge has a unique owner, these local rules
give one well-defined global edge-colouring of `G`. Every surviving triangle
is transversal inside its owner and has colour pattern red--red--blue, so no
triangle is monochromatic. Thus `G` does not arrow `(3,3)`.

The campaign's Theorem A says that every nonarrowing `n`-vertex graph satisfies
`beta(G)>=H(n)`: one adds the triangle-free edges to one colour class, takes an
independent `H(n)`-set in that triangle-free spanning graph, and obtains an
admissible set. Applying it to `G` proves the final assertion. ∎

## Adversarial dependency audit

1. **Owner part labels are coherent.** Each indexed owner receives one fixed
   labelled tripartition when it is constructed. Labels need not agree across
   owners because the first deletion leaves every edge with exactly one owner.

2. **No pre-deletion ambiguity survives.** The first deletion uses support
   co-membership, not edge provenance. Thus an edge is deleted whenever its
   endpoints co-lie in two indexed owner supports, even if one occurrence puts
   them in the same part or the two copies have different colours. Repeated
   identical indexed copies also count as distinct owners.

3. **Every final triangle is intrinsic.** The second deletion is applied to
   the complete triangle list after the first deletion and removes an edge
   from each triangle not contained in an owner. Choices are simultaneous;
   deletion cannot create a triangle. Hence any final triangle is contained in
   an owner.

4. **The red--red--blue pattern has no mixed-owner exception.** An edge that
   belonged to both pre-deletion colour families had endpoints in at least two
   owner supports and was deleted at step 1. Every final triangle's edges have
   the same unique owner: if its vertices lie in owner `i`, each edge's
   endpoints co-lie there, so no different owner can survive the uniqueness
   test. The triangle is transversal in that owner's three fixed parts.

5. **The conclusion matches #151 exactly.** In the campaign definitions,
   `beta(G)` is the maximum size of a set containing no nontrivial ambient
   maximal clique, `tau(G)=n-beta(G)`, and `H(n)` is the minimum independence
   number of an `n`-vertex triangle-free graph. The independently audited
   Theorem A proves `beta(G)>=H(n)` for every nonarrowing `G`, which is exactly
   `tau(G)<=n-H(n)`. No substitution of `tf_3`, asymptotic estimate, or Ramsey
   lower certificate is needed for this impossibility theorem.

The only external mathematical dependency is Theorem A; its full elementary
proof is reproduced in the cited campaign file and its independent audit.
The MSV dependencies are only the literal first- and second-deletion
definitions. Randomness and the probabilistic estimates are irrelevant.

## Scope and next representation

This theorem closes the **tripartite-owner counterexample lane**, not the MSV
paper, the Erdős--Rogers function, the general construction paradigm, or
Erdős #151.

More generally, unique owner plus destruction of extrinsic triangles lets
one paste together any good edge-colourings of the owner graphs. In fact, let
`E_i` be the final edges owned by indexed owner `i`. The `E_i` partition
`E(G)`, every final triangle lies in one `E_i`, and therefore

```text
G -> (3,3)  iff  some surviving owner graph (V_i,E_i) -> (3,3).
```

The forward implication follows by contraposition, pasting independent good
colourings of all nonarrowing owners. The reverse implication is immediate by
restriction. Similarly, if every surviving owner graph is `K_4`-free, then so
is `G`: the four triangles of a putative `K_4`, joined through shared edges,
would all have the same unique owner.

Thus replacing `T_3` by an arrowing graph is necessary but not sufficient:
the first and second deletions must preserve an arrowing subgraph inside at
least one owner. To escape this obstruction while retaining the same
deterministic `K_4`-free proof skeleton, the natural replacement owner is a
`K_4`-free edge-Folkman graph whose arrowing core is protected through both
deletions. This changes the representation and central lemma materially: the
search object becomes a packing of protected arrowing cores rather than a
random packing of tripartite graphs. It requires a fresh target-fidelity,
priority, and finite-feasibility audit before compute.
