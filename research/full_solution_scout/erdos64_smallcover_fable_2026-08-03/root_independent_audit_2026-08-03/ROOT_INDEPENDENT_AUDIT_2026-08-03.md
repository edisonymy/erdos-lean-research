# Independent hostile audit: the `sigma = 19` witness and the `sigma <= 18` exclusion

**Audit date:** 2026-08-03/04 UTC  
**Scope:** `sigma19_model.json`, the pure-incidence reduction and SAT
encoding, the available CNF/DRAT artifacts, and the priority claims in
`HANDOVER.md`, `REPORT.md`, and `ISSUE_UPDATE_DRAFT.md`.  
**Publication status:** this note is internal.  It is not a solution claim
and has not been posted, committed, or pushed by the auditor.

## Executive verdict

1. The 38-vertex model is valid: it is connected, simple, cubic,
   bipartite with sides 19+19, and has no `C4` or `C8`.  It has both a
   `C16` and a `C32`, so it is not an Erdős--Gyárfás counterexample.
2. The object is not new.  It is exactly Gordon Royle's explicit
   38-vertex graph posted on MathOverflow on 2 November 2009, and it is
   the known Foster-census graph `F038A`, the Levi graph of the cyclic
   `19_3` configuration generated by `{0,1,8}`.
3. The claim that the previous smallest known example had 70 vertices is
   false.  Royle supplied the order-38 example in 2009.  Moreover, the
   McKay--Afzaly public exact extremal table already records
   `ex_bip(37,{C4,C8}) = 54` and `ex_bip(38,{C4,C8}) = 57`, with one
   extremal graph at order 38.  Since minimum degree 3 requires at least
   `ceil(3n/2)` edges, that table already implies that the minimum *total
   order* is exactly 38.
4. The packet's count "38 hexagons" is not the ordinary unoriented simple
   cycle count.  There are exactly **19 unoriented simple 6-cycles**.  If
   38 was intended to use a rooted or oriented convention, that convention
   was not documented and must be labeled explicitly.
5. The pure-incidence reduction and the static `C8` encoding survived the
   hostile semantic audit.  The audit found no encoding unsoundness.
6. Fresh independent runs after the packet handover regenerated and
   certified every previously unavailable rung `sigma = 14,...,18`.
   Together with the hand proof at 4 and the independently replayed
   certificates at 5--13, the full computational ladder is now verified.
7. Subject to the audited encoding, the stronger statement **every such
   graph has at least 19 vertices in each bipartition class** is now
   certificate-backed.  The fresh 14--18 files currently live in a local
   audit directory and must be archived before a public reproducibility
   claim.
8. The useful result is not a new order-38 construction or a new
   minimum-order threshold.  It is an independently certified, stronger
   **part-size** statement.  Priority for that exact statement remains to
   be cleared.

## 1. Priority collision

### 1.1 Gordon Royle, 2009

Leah Wrenn Berman asked for small cubic bipartite graphs of girth at least
six with no 8-cycle.  Gordon Royle answered on 2 November 2009 with the
complete adjacency list of a "38-vertex bipartite cubic vertex-transitive
graph with no 4 or 8 cycles":

- <https://mathoverflow.net/questions/966/looking-for-cubic-bipartite-graphs-with-girth-at-least-six-and-no-cycles-of-len>

Tomaž Pisanski subsequently identified it on the same page as the Levi
graph of a self-dual, flag-transitive `19_3` configuration containing
triangles of lines but no quadrangles, and explicitly asked whether it was
smallest even without vertex-transitivity.  Thus both the example and the
minimum-order question were public by March 2010.

### 1.2 Peer-reviewed identification

G. Erskine, T. Griggs, and J. Širáň, "Colouring problems for symmetric
configurations with block size 3," *Journal of Combinatorial Designs* 29
(2021), 397--423, DOI <https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.21773>, identify the
same order-38 graph as the unique symmetric graph `F038A` in the Foster
census.  They state that its configuration is cyclic and generated by
the block `{0,1,8}` modulo 19.  Open-access version:

- <https://oro.open.ac.uk/75716/14/75716VOR.pdf>

### 1.3 The total-order threshold is already recorded

The McKay--Afzaly combinatorial-data page defines `H`-extremal graphs as
graphs avoiding every graph in `H`; `C_odd` enforces bipartiteness.  Its
exact table for `H={C_odd,C4,C8}` records:

- order 37: maximum 54 edges, one extremal graph;
- order 38: maximum 57 edges, one extremal graph.

The table's convention says that entries without a `>=` marker are exact.
Since a graph of minimum degree at least 3 on `n` vertices has at least
`ceil(3n/2)` edges, order 37 would require at least 56 edges and is
impossible, while the known cubic order-38 graph has 57 edges.  Therefore
the minimum total order 38 was already a direct consequence of this
public table:

- <https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/data/extremal.html>

This table does **not** by itself settle the stronger assertion that each
bipartition class has size at least 19 in graphs of arbitrary larger total
order.  That distinction is the only plausible novelty window left for
the pure-incidence computation.

A targeted search of configuration and Berge-`C4` hypergraph literature
found substantial adjacent work, including exact enumerations of
`v_3` configurations and asymptotic extremal results, but did not locate
the exact variable-line-size part-class theorem.  That is search-relative,
not a priority guarantee; direct specialist confirmation is still needed.

## 3. Semantic audit of the reduction and encoding

### 3.1 Graph-to-hypergraph reduction

Let `S` be the smaller side of a bipartite graph and let each vertex in
the other side define its neighborhood as a hyperedge on `S`.

- Minimum degree at least 3 gives point-degrees at least 3 and hyperedge
  sizes at least 3.
- `C4`-freeness is equivalent to linearity: two distinct hyperedges meet
  in at most one point.
- Every hyperedge contains at least three point pairs, so linearity gives
  at most `floor(binomial(|S|,2)/3)` hyperedges; unused encoded columns may
  therefore be padded.
- A simple incidence `C8` is equivalent, under linearity, to four distinct
  points whose four cyclic consecutive pairs lie on four distinct
  hyperedges.  The static quadrilateral clauses express exactly this.

The alias used to express an active line through its first three incidence
variables correctly enforces line size at least 3.  The `Y/Z/W/C3`
definitions were also checked against their intended Boolean meanings.

### 3.2 Independent finite equivalence checks

An independent enumerator compared the semantic incidence-graph test with
the static `C8` predicate on:

- all hypergraph families with lines of size at least 3 on 4 points;
- all such families on 5 points;
- all four-line, 3-uniform families on 8 points.

Across 42,878 linear families, including 15,960 positive `C8` cases,
there were zero mismatches.  See `c8_equivalence_audit.json`.

### 3.3 Symmetry breaking

The DoubleLex implementation was checked in two ways:

- 1,364 truth-table cases for the lexicographic auxiliary clauses, zero
  mismatches;
- all 4,096 binary `3 x 4` matrices, every row/column orbit having a
  DoubleLex representative.

The general soundness proof is short: choose a row/column permutation
whose row-major bitstring is lexicographically maximal; any inverted
adjacent row or column could be swapped to increase that bitstring.  The
sequential-counter auxiliaries preserve satisfiability.  This supports
soundness of the symmetry reduction, although it does not make the
auxiliary variables themselves syntactic automorphisms.

### 3.4 Limits of this audit

The audit establishes the intended mathematical reduction, checks the
load-bearing source paths, and exhaustively tests small semantic cases.  It
is not a second, independently implemented generator for the full
66,340-variable `sigma = 18` formula.  Publication should therefore bind
the exact generator revision, exact CNF hash, proof hash, and checker hash,
and ideally add an independently generated LRAT or a second encoder.

## 5. What may safely be claimed

Safe now:

- the 38-vertex model is a correct copy of the known graph `F038A`;
- it has 19 unoriented 6-cycles, no 4- or 8-cycles, and does have 16- and
  32-cycles;
- the pure-incidence encoding has survived a substantive independent
  soundness audit;
- exact rungs 5--18 have independently checked UNSAT certificates in the
  present audit state (with 4 handled by hand);
- the order-38 threshold is known from prior public data and is independently
  re-derived by this campaign.

Not safe now:

- "new 38-vertex graph";
- "previous smallest known example was 70";
- "new extremal number" for minimum total order;
- "38 hexagons" as an ordinary unoriented simple-cycle count;
- "reproducibly archived DRAT-certified through sigma 18" until the fresh
  14--18 pairs are moved out of the temporary audit directory and deposited;
- any implication that this resolves Erdős problem #64.

Potentially useful after completing the gate:

- a reproducible independent certificate for the stronger part-size
  theorem, "both bipartition classes have size at least 19," explicitly
  presented as stronger than the known total-order threshold and subjected
  to a targeted priority check.

## 6. Publication gate

Before any public issue update, preprint, or Zenodo record makes the
part-size claim:

1. Archive the freshly regenerated exact `sigma = 14,15,16,17,18` CNFs,
   proofs, and JSON transcripts; do not leave the only copies in a
   temporary audit directory.
2. Record SHA-256 hashes for every CNF, proof, generator, solver, and
   checker.  Archive the large files themselves on Zenodo; hashes of
   missing files are not reproducibility.
3. Replay every proof with the bound checker binary and retain its complete
   output.  Prefer an LRAT conversion plus a second checker if practical.
4. Run a second independent encoder or a model-enumeration cross-check at
   the largest feasible rung.
5. Cite Royle 2009, Pisanski's identification, Erskine--Griggs--Širáň
   2021, and the McKay--Afzaly extremal table prominently.
6. Ask Gordon Royle, Brendan McKay, Tomaž Pisanski, or another configuration
   specialist whether the exact *part-size* statement has appeared before.
7. Describe the result as a certificate-backed strengthening/reconfirmation,
   not as a new order-38 construction or new minimum-order value, unless a
   priority expert supplies contrary evidence.
