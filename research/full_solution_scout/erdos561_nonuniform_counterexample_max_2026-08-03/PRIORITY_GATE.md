# Erdős #561 nonuniform counterexample lane: priority gate

Date of gate: 2026-08-03

## Gate verdict

`LAUNCH`, narrowly for

\[
F_R=K_{1,2}\sqcup K_{1,1}=P_3\sqcup K_2,
\qquad
F_B=2K_{1,2}=2P_3.
\]

Here “nonuniform” means that this ordered pair is not a pair of uniform
star forests: the red forest has degree sequence `(2,1)`.  The blue forest
is uniform.  This is not the old symmetric `(2,1)` versus `(2,1)` probe.

For red degrees `(2,1)` and blue degrees `(2,2)`, the conjectured formula is

* `l_2 = 2+2-1 = 3`;
* `l_3 = max(2+2-1, 1+2-1) = 3`;
* `l_4 = 1+2-1 = 2`;

and hence predicts size-Ramsey number `8`.  Therefore any graph with at most
seven edges that arrows `(P3 disjoint-union K2, 2P3)` is a full
counterexample to the universal conjecture in Erdős problem #561.

The finite witness class is launch-grade.  After deleting irrelevant
isolated vertices, every connected component with `m <= 7` edges has at
most `m+1 <= 8` vertices.  All connected types with at most seven vertices
occur in the NetworkX graph atlas; an eight-vertex connected graph with
seven edges is necessarily a tree, and all 23 such types are generated by
`nonisomorphic_trees(8)`.  Component multisets then give every isolate-free
host with at most seven edges.  Each has at most 128 edge-colourings.  This
is far below the one-CPU-day launch ceiling.

## Family-by-family exclusion

The audit used theorem statements in primary sources, not only the live
problem-page summary.

* Burr--Erdős--Faudree--Rousseau--Schelp's original exact result has both
  forests uniform.  The red target `(2,1)` is not uniform.
* Cheng, Theorem 14, covers the all-odd case and the case in which one whole
  target is a matching.  This tuple has degree 2 and neither target is a
  matching.
* Cheng, Theorem 18, assumes every entry after the first is 1 on both sides
  (plus its ordering hypotheses).  The blue tail entry is 2.
* Győri--Schelp's numerical sufficient condition fails at the final layer:
  `l_4=2`, so `binom(l_4,2)=1` cannot exceed the tail sum `2` (and also
  fails the weak-inequality paraphrase appearing in one later source).
* Davoodi--Javadi--Kamranian--Raeisi, Theorem 2.3, has a single star on one
  side and all stars on the other of degree at least 2.  Both targets here
  have two components.
* Their Theorem 2.4 covers `2K_{1,n}` versus an arbitrary star forest only
  when every degree in the other forest is at least 2.  Orienting the blue
  target as `2K_{1,2}`, the red target contains a degree-1 star.
* Their Theorems 2.5 and 2.6 fail respectively because not all degrees are
  odd, and because the degree/lower-bound hypotheses on the nonuniform side
  fail.
* Fu--Luo--Ni arXiv:2606.04439v1 claimed the arbitrary conjecture, but that
  result was removed wholesale in v2.  The current v3 is about Ramsey-minimal
  graphs for uniform star forests and supplies no theorem for this tuple.
  A withdrawn v1 claim is not treated as coverage.
* The 1981 Ramsey-minimal-graph paper studies Ramsey-finiteness/minimality and
  does not supply a hidden size formula covering this tuple.

Exact-expression and notation searches on 2026-08-03 for variants of
`(P3 union K2, 2P3)`, `(K_{1,2} union K_{1,1}, 2K_{1,2})`, and their
size-Ramsey number returned no exact-value result.  Searches of GitHub and
the arXiv record exposed no code or unpublished computation for this tuple.
The live page still labels #561 open, but that database status is supporting
context only, not the basis of the gate.

After the first tuple returned a clean null result and passed an independent
catalogue audit, the campaign authorized one capped meta-sweep over all
asymmetric two-component tuples with formula at most 10.  A programmatic
family audit found exactly two further uncovered color-swap classes:
`(2,1)` versus `(3,2)`, with formula 9, and `(2,2)` versus `(3,1)`, with
formula 10.  The same primary-source exclusions above apply.  Exact-notation
searches on 2026-08-03 for both pairs found no exact value; the only close
search result was a paper on the *connected* size-Ramsey number of matchings
versus `2P3`, which is a different parameter and different target pair.
This second exact-notation search occurred after the theorem-family filter
and computation, so it is recorded as a post-computation novelty check, not
misdescribed as a pre-launch gate.

## Claim discipline

This gate is a priority assessment, not a novelty proof.  A found host must
pass two definition-level verifiers before any mathematical claim is made.
A null search is only a bounded negative result for this tuple and does not
resolve Erdős #561.
